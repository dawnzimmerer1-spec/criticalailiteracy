Conrad & Kamperman Critical AI Literacy Approach

To help educators situate AI within political, economic, environmental, cultural, historical, and ethical systems before deciding whether or how to adopt it.

Overview

What the framework is trying to do

Conrad and Kamperman's approach pushes against AI literacy that becomes simple upskilling. It asks educators to examine the systems hidden beneath frictionless AI interfaces, including labor, corporate power, environmental cost, intellectual property, data sovereignty, and epistemic justice.

A particularly important feature is that critical judgment can lead to limits, resistance, or non-use. Competence with AI is subordinate to educational, ethical, and social judgment.

Framework structure

Core dimensions or competencies

Political and economic context

Corporate influence, technological determinism, ownership, incentives, and institutional governance.

Labor and environment

Human labor behind AI systems and their material environmental costs.

Knowledge and justice

Epistemic justice, representation, intellectual property, and diversity of thought.

Human agency

Using, limiting, modifying, questioning, or refusing AI depending on context.

Practical use

Especially useful for

Faculty development, institutional policy critique, teaching philosophy, course design, AI governance discussions, and critical technology studies.

Evaluation

Strengths and limitations

Strengths

  • Strong critique of technological inevitability and AI hype.
  • Treats non-use and refusal as legitimate literate choices.
  • Connects classroom decisions to political economy and institutional governance.

Limitations

  • Primarily conceptual and pedagogical rather than a validated competency assessment.
  • Less structured as a step-by-step curriculum framework than UNESCO or OU.

Portability

How well does it travel?

Moderate–High. The questions travel broadly across higher education, though implementation requires educators to translate them into local curricula and policies.
